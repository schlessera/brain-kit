A scoped context briefing's coverage now says which source lines a widened excerpt holds. When a hit is widened to a folded chunk's span (the sections the indexer merged into one chunk), its coverage excerpt carries `lines: [start, end]`, the 1-based file lines from the span's first heading to its last line. Every other excerpt has no `lines` field. `budgetExhausted` is now also true when a hit kept its snippet because its section or span did not fit, so coverage separates a budget fallback from a span that could not be placed. A chunk made of whole sections widens; a piece of a split section keeps its snippet. The span is found by chunking a run of whole sections again and getting the hit's chunk back exactly, at exactly one place in the file, so the indexer's own rules decide what the chunk holds. A short lead folded forward is part of the span. A level-one heading inside a section, a heading repeated elsewhere in the file, and a heading with a closing `##` no longer affect it. A match that only one section of the chunk holds takes that section, even if another chunk repeats the line. A section that has the match line as one of its own lines takes precedence over one that only contains that text inside a longer line.
