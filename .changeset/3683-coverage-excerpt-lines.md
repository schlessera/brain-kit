A scoped context briefing's coverage now says which source lines a widened excerpt holds. When a hit is widened to a folded chunk's span (the sections the indexer merged into one chunk), its coverage excerpt carries `lines: [start, end]`, the 1-based file lines from the span's first heading to its last line. Every other excerpt has no `lines` field. `budgetExhausted` is now also true when a hit kept its snippet because its section or span did not fit, so coverage separates a budget fallback from a span that could not be placed. The index now records each chunk's source lines (schema 16, `chunks.start_line` and `chunks.end_line`), and the span is read from them instead of being inferred. A hit widens only when those lines are exactly a run of whole sections. A piece of a section split over several chunks records no lines and keeps its snippet, as does a chunk whose file changed since the last index run. The chunker version is bumped, so the first `brain index` after upgrading re-chunks every markdown document. Chunks whose heading and text are unchanged keep their rows and vectors, with only their lines updated. A level-one heading inside a section, a heading repeated elsewhere in the file, a heading with a closing `##`, and sections whose text is repeated under other headings no longer affect it. An empty `##` heading inside a section no longer lets another section's lines decide which section a hit expands to. A match that only one section of the chunk holds takes that section, even if another chunk repeats the line. A section that has the match line as one of its own lines takes precedence over one that only contains that text inside a longer line.
