The governance policy accepts a top-level `google` block (#4204), the settings the Google host tools will decide with: `enabled`, the gog `account` alias, `services`, a mode per effect class (`read: deny | allow`, `reversible: deny | ask | allow`, `irreversible: deny | ask`), per-operation overrides, `gmail.send_to`, `gmail.max_recipients`, `gmail.hidden_labels`, `standalone.reversible` and `limits` (`items_per_call` up to 25, `reversible_per_turn`). Absent, or without `enabled: true`, Google is disabled. The block is strict: `irreversible: allow`, `allow` on an irreversible operation, `ask` on a read, `account: auto`, an address as the account, a malformed `send_to` entry, a label name in `hidden_labels` and any unknown key, `calendar` included, make the policy invalid. `brain policy check --json` reports the resolved block as `effective.google` and each error with its path, such as `google.modes.irreversible`. The internal `@schlessera/brain-common/internal/google-operations` entry adds the block's schema and defaults and `decideGoogleCall`, the pure decision function every front door will use: it refuses, admits without a card (by policy, a remembered approval while untainted, or a session allowance while tainted) or asks with a card offering the standing choice the plan allows, in the order of the plan's decision procedure, and counts every missing host fact against the call.
